# Agent Wolf — a note for testers

Thanks for trying this. It is early, it will have rough edges, and telling me about them is the
whole point.

Read this once before you sign in. It is five minutes and it will save you being confused later.

---

## What this is

You have a hunch about a market. *"If interest rates keep falling, housebuilder shares will
rise."* Right now that hunch lives in your head, it is vague, and six months later you can't
honestly say whether you were right — because you never wrote down what "right" would have looked
like.

Agent Wolf makes you write it down, and then checks it for you every day.

## What it is not

- **It is not advice, and it will never tell you to buy or sell anything.** It tells you whether
  the thing *you* said would happen is happening. Please don't put money behind it — certainly
  not at this stage, when I'm still finding out whether it works at all.
- **It is not a prediction engine.** It has no opinion about the future. It only checks your
  opinion against what actually happened.
- **It is not fast.** It thinks once a day. See below.

## How it goes, start to finish

**1. You sign in with Google.** Only people I've added by name can get in. If it refuses you,
that's me not having added you yet — tell me.

**2. You state your hunch**, in your own words. One or two sentences. Don't try to be precise
yet; that's the next step's job.

**3. It interviews you.** This is a conversation, and it is the part that actually matters. It
will push you to say things like: *which* housebuilders, measured how, over what period, and —
the important one — **what would have to happen for you to admit you were wrong?**

That last question is the whole idea. A hunch you can't lose is not a hunch, it's a mood. Expect
the interview to be slightly annoying. That's it working.

**4. You press go.** At that moment the scoreboard locks: the exact measurements and the exact
deadline are fixed, and can't be quietly edited later to make you look better. That is deliberate
and it is the point.

**5. Then you wait.** Once a day, early morning, it goes and looks at real market data, writes up
what it found, and updates a score. **Nothing happens instantly.** If you state something today,
come back tomorrow.

**6. It taps you on the shoulder** when either something you said would happen *has* happened, or
your deadline has passed. It won't decide for you. You look at the evidence and say: I was right,
I was wrong, or actually my idea needs changing.

## Things that will confuse you, listed in advance

- **The score is a summary, not a verdict.** There's a number between -1 and +1 showing roughly
  how much the evidence is leaning your way. It never decides anything by itself. Only the
  specific conditions you set can do that.
- **Nothing visible happens for the first 24 hours.** Not broken. Waiting for the morning.
- **The research is done by an AI reading market data. It can be wrong.** Treat its write-ups as
  a keen assistant's notes, not as fact. If it says something that looks wrong, that is exactly
  the kind of thing I want to hear about.
- **Once it's live, you can't edit the scoreboard.** If your idea genuinely needs changing, the
  app has a proper route for that — it proposes an amendment and you accept it. What you can't do
  is quietly move the goalposts.
- **You can't undo a final verdict.** Confirmed, wrong, and retired are all permanent. If you want
  to run a changed version, you start a new one, and it remembers what it came from.

## What is stored, and who can see it

- **Your hypotheses, the interview conversation, and the research write-ups** are stored on a
  server I run, in the UK/EU. I can read them. No one else can — there is no public page and no
  sharing feature.
- **Your Google account gives us your email address and nothing else.** No contacts, no files, no
  calendar. It is only used to check you're on the list.
- **Market data comes from public sources.** Nothing you type is sent to a market-data provider.
- **Nothing is sold, shared or used for advertising**, and there is no analytics or tracking on
  the page.
- **Ask me to delete your stuff at any point and I will**, no explanation needed.

## When it breaks

It will. When it does, the useful thing is not "it's broken" but:

- roughly what time it was,
- what you had just clicked,
- and what you expected to happen instead.

A screenshot beats all three. Don't worry about whether it's a real bug — send it anyway, I'd
much rather sift than miss things.

## What I'd most like to know

Bug reports are welcome, but these are more valuable:

1. **Did the interview make your idea sharper, or did it just make you tired?** This is the part
   I'm least sure about.
2. **Was the daily write-up worth reading?** Or did you skim it and look at the number?
3. **When it tapped you on the shoulder, did you have enough to make the call?**
4. **Would you have kept using it if I hadn't asked you to?** Say no if it's no. That's the most
   useful answer on this list.

Thank you — genuinely. Feedback on something half-built is worth ten times feedback on something
finished.
